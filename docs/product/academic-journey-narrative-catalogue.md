# Academic journeys: interlocking narrative catalogue

Status: **Proposed discovery baseline, v0.5 — stories to develop, not delivered functionality or executed tests.**
Date: 14 November 2026. Suggested coordinating owner: Academic Registry, with academic, student, research and service representatives.

## Purpose and reading guide

These independent stories describe the university from several viewpoints: an applicant choosing a university, a lecturer creating a module, a researcher completing a doctorate, and the teams that make each journey possible. Together they follow provision from its first proposal through recruitment, study, awards, review and eventual closure. They also follow the records, decisions and services that connect those experiences.

The initial deliverable is a **catalogue of 40 narratives**, rather than 40 fully written workflows. Each row gives the starting situation, the main events and a complication, the intended outcome, and links to other stories. A story can be developed independently using a stated starting snapshot. When stories are joined, they must share the same people, programme versions, dates, decisions and identifiers; another story's outcome becomes the next story's starting fact.

Coverage is checked against the repository's [enterprise reference model v3.0](../reference/revelation-student-records-enterprise-reference-model-3.0.json): **33 systems/actors and 69 interactions**. Sections below assign every reference interaction to a narrative and separately identify interactions missing from, or insufficiently detailed in, that model. Assignment means **planned narrative coverage**, not that an integration exists, a contract has been agreed or a journey has passed a test. Further interactions will emerge during walkthroughs.

Use the [current capability matrix](current-capabilities.md) for implementation status, the [business process inventory](../business-processes/process-inventory.md) for existing process identifiers, and the [curriculum management requirements](../requirements/curriculum-management-system.md) for the proposed authoring and approval capability. The reference model's broad “CM implemented” annotation must not be read as delivery of those new requirements.

All people, courses and events introduced here are fictional. Rules, deadlines, credit structures, eligibility and reporting routes are configurable story fixtures to agree with institutional owners, not assertions about current national policy or external service APIs. “Course” means a programme of study here; a module is a unit within it. A diet defines the permitted combinations and sequences of modules leading to an award. PGT means postgraduate taught study; PGR means postgraduate research.

## Shared setting and how the stories connect

The setting is fictional **Northbridge University** and its teaching/research partners. Dr Aisha Patel proposes a BSc in Environmental Data Science. Its shared modules also serve other programmes. Sam considers the university, visits, applies through UCAS, meets an offer, joins the course and ultimately graduates. Jo enters a part-time route with prior learning; Mei joins a taught master's; Ada is an apprentice; Morgan is a funded doctoral researcher and later a teaching assistant. Additional students provide the partner, mobility, placement and short-course perspectives. These are separate lives: one student need not experience every exception.

Stories use a weekly relative timeline in which **t is the start of the academic year for the entry cohort**, counted in calendar weeks; the full [timeline convention](academic-journeys/README.md#timeline-convention) is defined once, with the expanded journeys. Each cohort's anchor is an exact date: for the 2030 entry cohort it is Monday 23 September 2030. PGR, part-time and other cohorts need their own explicitly dated anchors and calendars. Interruption, repeat study and a new curriculum release must not silently move someone onto a different cohort's rules.

| A funded researcher becomes a graduate and colleague | AJ-30 → AJ-31 ↔ AJ-32 → AJ-33 → AJ-29 | Studentship, candidature, supervision, progress milestones, separate employment/payment facts, examination and research award. |
| Institutional services sustain all journeys | AJ-34–AJ-40 intersect the relevant stories | Reporting snapshots, data-quality findings, communication receipts, access rights, archived evidence, integration acknowledgements and audit history. |

Independent execution requires a small synthetic starting pack, not replay of every preceding story. For example, AJ-25 starts with a fixed curriculum/rule version, confirmed mark set and support outcomes; it need not run admissions first. A connected run must instead consume those artefacts from the preceding narratives. Alternatives such as a deferred applicant and a student who enrols immediately are separate branches, never simultaneous states of the same enrolment.

## Architecture and ownership rules used by every story

1. **Keep process ownership and confirmed outcomes separate.** Curriculum management owns curriculum proposals; SRS holds accepted operational versions and student bindings. Finance, HR and research systems retain their own authority. Receiving their outcome does not transfer ownership of their entire ledger or case history to SRS.
2. **Treat the reference model as logical.** The optional internal curriculum module shares the modular-monolith deployment. Replacing it with an external application changes the adapter, not the meaning of an approved release. Exam scheduling and integrity can also be internal capabilities. A logical exchange need not be a network call.
3. **Respect the attendance implementation distinction.** The reference interaction F-AM-SIS-01 describes attendance records/alerts. Revelation's native attendance module owns evidence and casework and supplies confirmed engagement outcomes. AJ-20/23/39 must exercise the native boundary and separately specify any external-provider variant; the reference wording does not authorise a new raw-feed ingestion path into core.
4. **Distribute support effects through SRS.** Wellbeing owns evidence and decisions. Necessary approved adjustment effects reach VLE, attendance and examinations through SRS; detailed medical evidence does not follow the same route. Exceptional circumstances remain a separate determination and board consideration.
5. **Keep governance actors distinct from software.** The external examiner and exam board exercise academic judgement. A scheduler, analytical flag, delivery receipt or workflow timer cannot ratify a result or confer an award.
6. **Preserve legitimate ecosystem flows.** VLE→BI, AM→BI, DW→BI and CRM→EWP are reference-context exchanges, not automatically SRS-owned integrations. ESB has no assigned reference flows; AJ-39/40 exercise it as an optional routing/operational pattern, not a newly invented source of academic truth.
7. **Use explicit states, dates and receipts.** Draft, submitted, approved, published, effective, delivered and acknowledged mean different things. A successful send is not evidence of application by the receiving service. Authorised decisions remain attributable when a workflow is resumed, upgraded or replaced.

## How to expand and validate each narrative

For each AJ identifier, create a readable story first, followed by a short structured walkthrough. Record:

- The protagonist's goal and context; other actors, their responsibilities and permissions; the starting snapshot and what the protagonist already knows.
- Programme/module/offering/cohort and rule versions, relevant identities, academic period and dates. Record both the date an event takes effect and when it is recorded where these differ.
- Chronological scenes: give the planned week or window relative to the declared academic-year start (t), what each person does and sees, the decision/gate, data used, owning system and evidence left behind. Include communications, waiting, rework and the student's experience of service hand-offs. Preserve actual dates and within-week ordering separately; a planned week is not proof that a gate completed.
- Each interaction's reference flow or GI item, sender/receiver, minimum payload, authority, trigger, correlation/idempotency key, expected acknowledgement and failure/reconciliation route. Specify a logical internal hand-off, external API/file, manual task or ecosystem-only exchange rather than assuming one transport.
- A normal outcome and meaningful variants: rejected/returned/deferred/cancelled decisions; missing or conflicting evidence; late/duplicate/out-of-order exchange; disputed decisions; accessibility needs; and recovery. Include only relevant variants, using separate branches where the same person cannot experience both.
- Observable end states, owned unresolved tasks, a record of who decided what and why, and links to the narratives that can consume those outcomes.
- Applicable business-process and requirement IDs, missing requirements, implementation status from evidence, institutional policy decisions and named reviewers. Do not equate a written narrative with an accepted institutional workflow.

Walk through the scenes with students, academic owners, Registry and service owners. At each boundary ask **who else needs to act, know or acknowledge this**, and what happens if they cannot. Add discoveries to GI with an owner; then decide whether to extend an existing process/contract, propose a new reference interaction or record a deliberate exclusion. Recheck coverage whenever the reference model or a narrative changes.
