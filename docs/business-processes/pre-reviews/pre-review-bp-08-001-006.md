## Summary

ADR-021's entities all exist, but they are mostly recording surfaces, not controls:

- **Restrictions and holds:** recorded, but nothing outside the rights service enforces them.
- **Disposals:** recorded, but none is executed.
- **The audit hash chain:** leaves out the fields that matter most.
- **The legacy retention sweep:** irreversibly anonymises people while ignoring holds, restrictions, open rights requests and the configured schedules. That is the most serious gap.

None of this is unlawful today, because there are no production tenants. Each item must be fixed before any institution's live data is loaded.

Findings: **1 Blocker, 9 Major, 3 Minor, 2 Observations.**

## Findings

| # | Severity | Finding | Evidence | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R08-1 | Blocker (pre-production) | **The retention sweep bypasses holds and governance.** `POST /admin/retention/enforce?dryRun=false` anonymises everyone whose last enrolment ended more than 6 years ago (hard-coded). It never reads `record_hold`, `processing_restriction`, open `individual_rights_request` rows or `retention_schedule`, and it writes no `record_disposition`. It ignores the register's 7-year finance rows, such as SLC overpayment. `system-administrator` alone can run it, so there is no approval separate from execution (BP-08-005 steps 4 and 5). Erasing data that is under a live access request can be an offence under DPA 2018 s.173. | `retention-service.ts`; `routes/admin-retention.ts`; `permissions.ts` line 179 | Disable apply mode until it is driven by `retention_assignment`, blocked by holds, open rights requests and restrictions, approved by a records manager other than the executor, and writes a disposition certificate. |
| R08-2 | Major | **Anonymisation is incomplete.** Only `person_identity` and `student_address` are anonymised. It leaves `student_contact_method`, `disability_declaration`, the `exceptional_circumstances.notes` narrative, `application_received_payload`, HESA and regulatory record payloads, `integration_exchange.payload_summary` (the SLC reference), `delivery_item.payload`, `document_issue.content_snapshot`, and everything held by the wellbeing and attendance modules and the documents store. Award holders keep all their contact data indefinitely (they are flagged only). | `retention-service.ts`; schemas | Build a per-record-class disposal inventory across core, modules and stores. Minimise award holders to what the permanent award record needs (name, date of birth, award). |
| R08-3 | Major | **Restriction markers are not enforced.** `processing_restriction` is read and written only by `rights-request-service.ts`. No API, workflow, extract, event or integration consults it. ADR-021 requires it to be an enforceable marker. Art. 18 restriction allows storage only, unless consent or another listed exception applies. | `grep processingRestrictions` (only 1 service file) | Add a restriction guard to outbound delivery, regulatory extraction, communications and automated decisions. Distinguish restriction types by their effect, and propagate them to targets (ADR-019). |
| R08-4 | Major | **Disposal is a record, not an execution.** `recordDisposition` checks holds on that one assignment, then inserts a row. Nothing is deleted or verified. `scheduledDisposalDate` is typed in by hand, not calculated from the schedule's trigger event. The same permission approves and executes. | `rights-request-service.ts` `recordDisposition`, `assignSchedule` | Calculate dates from lifecycle events. Execute through target work items with acknowledgements (ADR-019). The certificate records the class, the count, the targets and the approver, never the content. |
| R08-5 | Major | **The DSAR clock and decision are not modelled.** `statutoryDeadlineDate` is supplied by the caller. There are no fields for the identity-verified date, a clarification pause (stop-the-clock, in force 2026-02-05), an Art. 12(3) extension of up to two further months with its reason, the delivery date, exemptions per item, or third-party redaction. `rights_decision` has no reasons and no exemption code. Nothing stops the opener deciding. `decide()` does not close the case. Every request type is opened under process `BP-08-003`, including erasure, rectification and restriction, which belong to BP-08-004. Core has no disclosure package; only wellbeing has an export. | `rights.ts`; `rights-request-service.ts` `openRequest`, `decide` | Compute the deadline from receipt (or ID verification), with pause and extension events. Add reasons and exemption codes to the decision. Route each type to its process. Add an assembled disclosure manifest. |
| R08-6 | Major | **There is no data-protection complaint route.** Under the DUAA, from 2026-06-19, controllers must facilitate complaints, acknowledge them within 30 days, and respond without undue delay. There is no complaint request type or case. | `rights.ts` `requestTypeCode` comment; CMS commencement note | Add a `complaint` request type with its acknowledgement and response deadlines, or document that the institution's complaints system owns it. |
| R08-7 | Major | **Audit tamper evidence is partial.** The hash covers only tenant, entity, field, action, actor and time. `before_value`, `after_value`, `reason_code`, `reason_text`, `correlation_id`, `workflow_instance_id` and `actor_display_name` can be changed without detection. The chain head is read without a lock, so concurrent writes fork the chain. There is no verification routine. `sealPartition` takes the last row after `rangeStart` with no upper bound, so a seal is not a seal of its range. Nothing anchors the chain outside the database. | `audit/service.ts` `record`, `sealPartition` | Hash the canonical full row. Serialise per chain with an advisory lock. Add verify-chain and verify-seal jobs that raise a control incident (ADR-021). Bound the seal at `rangeEnd`. Anchor seal hashes externally, for example in the log stack. |
| R08-8 | Major | **There is no segregation of duty in audit review.** Opening an audit-review case and adding findings need only `audit-log:read`, which `registry-administrator` holds, so the people being audited can review themselves and a read permission authorises writes. Sealing uses `retention:enforce`. | `routes/audit-review.ts`; `permissions.ts` lines 178–179 | Add `audit-review:manage` for DPO and security roles only. Block a reviewer from recording findings on their own actions. Add a distinct `audit:seal` permission. |
| R08-9 | Major | **Identity merges are not governed.** `linkPersons` creates a link and redirect without any decision. `decide()` does not check that the decider differs from the opener, or that the survivor is one of the candidates. There is no handling for conflicting statutory identifiers (HUSID, ULN, UCAS Personal ID, SLC SSN) or for a reversible link before a full merge. Redirects are consumed by nothing. A wrong merge leads to misdirected disclosure, which is a personal-data breach. | `identity-resolution-service.ts`; capability limitation | Allow links only from an authorised decision, and enforce decider ≠ opener. Add the identifier-conflict state, consumer redirect contracts and an unmerge. |
| R08-10 | Major | **The data-correction case is an empty shell.** `data_correction_case` holds only the entity type and field name. It has no requested value, evidence reference, effective period, classification (correction versus current change), impact on submitted returns, or decision. | `identity-resolution.ts` `dataCorrectionCases` | Add these fields, and link the impact to BP-07-008 when a submitted return is affected. |
| R08-11 | Minor | **Special-category reads are barely audited.** Core has only 4 read-audit call sites (`actionType: 'read'`). Ethnicity is not read-audited, although `person_identity` says "read audit applies", and neither are EC notes or adjustments. | `grep "actionType: *'read'"` | Read-audit every special-category projection through a shared hook. |
| R08-12 | Minor | **The data-subject register is inaccurate and stale.** It gives ethnicity's basis as "Legal Obligation (Equality Act)". Special-category data needs an Art. 9 condition, likely DPA 2018 Sch. 1 para 8 (equality) or para 4 (statistics), and HESA's sensitive fields allow "information refused". The HESA ID is set to "study + 6 years", but the HUSID is re-used for later study. The coverage matrix dated 2026-06-04 lists 7 register updates still outstanding. | [Register](../../requirements/data-subject-register.md); [matrix](../../architecture/data-subject-coverage-matrix.md) | Record Art. 6 and Art. 9 bases separately, and review the identifier retention. The DPO must approve the changes. |

| R08-15 | Observation | **The process pages are stale.** Their BR-3 rows say that rights decisions, restriction markers and audit review do not exist; all three now exist as data. | Pages BR-3 | Refresh BR-3 when the pages are next revised. |
