# Domain Glossary

> Status: Draft — Phase 1
> Last updated: 2026-06-04
> This glossary defines the authoritative meaning of every domain term used in Revelation SRS. All documentation, data models, API schemas, and code must use these terms consistently. UK HE conventions throughout.

---

## A

**Academic Integrity**
The set of values and regulations governing honest conduct in academic work. Breaches include plagiarism, collusion, contract cheating, and exam misconduct. Managed through a formal investigation and panel process; outcomes are recorded by the SIS. See also: *Misconduct*.

**Academic Year**
The annual cycle of teaching, assessment, and administration, typically running from September to August in UK HE. Divided into periods (terms or semesters). The academic year is the primary temporal reference for enrolment, module delivery, and statutory reporting.

**Adjustment** — see *Reasonable Adjustment*

**Admissions**
The process of receiving, evaluating, and deciding on applications for enrolment. In UK HE, undergraduate admissions are typically managed through UCAS. The admissions decision (offer, conditional offer, rejection) is the precursor to enrolment.

**Applicant**
An individual who has applied for a place on a programme but is not yet enrolled. Applicants become students on completion of enrolment.

**Assessment**
A task or series of tasks through which a student's attainment of module learning outcomes is measured. May be formative (ungraded feedback) or summative (graded, counts toward the final module mark). Assessment types include: written examination, coursework, portfolio, practical, presentation, viva voce.

**Assessment Component**
A single graded element within a module's assessment structure (e.g. "Essay 40%, Exam 60%"). A module result is derived by aggregating components according to configured weightings.

**Award**
The formal qualification conferred on a student who has met all the requirements of a programme. In UK HE: Certificate, Diploma, Bachelor's degree (with or without honours), Integrated Master's, Master's, Doctorate, etc.

**Award Board** — see *Exam Board*

---

## B

**Bitemporal**
A data storage model maintaining two independent time axes for each record:
- **Valid time**: when the fact was true in the real world.
- **Transaction time**: when the fact was recorded in the system.
Enables reconstruction of what the system believed at any past point, and what the real-world truth was at any past point. Required for regulatory returns, board paper reconstruction, and audit.

**Board of Examiners** — see *Exam Board*

**Bursary**
A financial award made to a student, typically means-tested or merit-based, that does not require repayment. Distinct from a *Loan*.

---

## C

**Candidature**
The status and period of being a registered *Postgraduate Research (PGR)* student working towards a research degree, from initial registration through to thesis submission. Candidature is subject to periodic review (see PGR progress milestones) and can be extended, suspended or ended by formal decision.

**CRM — Customer Relationship Management (system)**
A system that manages an institution's interactions with prospects, applicants, students and alumni — recruitment communications, engagement tracking and similar. Distinct from the SIS, which is the authoritative record of enrolment and academic outcomes; the CRM may supply application data or receive status updates, but does not hold the authoritative student record.

**CAS — Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies**
A reference number generated by an institution holding a Student sponsor licence, assigned to an international student who requires a Student visa. The student uses the CAS reference to apply for their visa from UKVI. The SIS is responsible for generating CAS creation requests and maintaining CAS records.

**Classification** — see *Degree Classification*

**CRIS — Current Research Information System**
A specialist system that holds a researcher's profile, publications, and research activity, distinct from and complementary to the SIS's record of a *Postgraduate Research (PGR)* student's enrolment and progression milestones.

**Cohort**
A group of students who begin a programme in the same academic year and are expected to progress through it together. Cohort membership is used in reporting, benchmarking, and some progression rule applications.

**Compensation**
A regulatory mechanism by which a student who narrowly fails one or more modules is nonetheless permitted to progress or be awarded, on the basis that their overall performance across the programme is sufficient. Rules for compensation are configured per institution and programme.

**Condonement**
Similar to *Compensation*; a decision by the Exam Board to overlook a marginal fail in specific circumstances. Institutions use either or both terms; the SIS supports both as configurable board decision types.

**Conditional Offer**
An admissions offer contingent on the applicant meeting specified conditions (typically A-level grades or equivalent). The offer lapses if conditions are not met; it converts to an unconditional offer (and proceeds to enrolment) when conditions are confirmed.

**Credit**
A unit of academic volume representing a defined amount of student learning effort. UK HE uses the CATS (Credit Accumulation and Transfer Scheme) framework. A standard full-time undergraduate year is 120 credits; an honours degree is 360 credits.

**Credit Framework**
The credit structure governing a programme: total credits required, the level distribution (Level 4, 5, 6 for undergraduate), and minimum credit thresholds per level required for progression and award.

**CQFW — Credit and Qualifications Framework for Wales**
The Welsh equivalent of the English/NI credit and level framework (see *Credit Framework*, *Level*), used to describe the volume and level of Welsh-medium and Wales-regulated programmes in statutory returns to *Medr*.

---

## D

**Data Subject Access Request (DSAR)**
A formal request by an individual (data subject) under UK GDPR Article 15 to receive a copy of the personal data held about them. The SIS must be able to produce a complete, accurate disclosure including temporal history and audit records.

**Degree Classification**
The honours grade awarded to a student who completes an undergraduate honours programme, derived from their marks across the final year(s) according to the institution's classification algorithm. Standard UK classifications: First Class (1st), Upper Second (2:1), Lower Second (2:2), Third Class (3rd), Pass (unclassified).

**DfE — Department for the Economy (Northern Ireland)**
The Northern Ireland Executive department responsible for higher education policy and funding in Northern Ireland, and the recipient of Northern Ireland's statutory HE return. Not to be confused with England's Department for Education, which shares the same abbreviation but has no equivalent statutory-return role in this model.

**Diet — see *Module Diet***

**Disability Declaration**
A student's formal disclosure of a disability or long-term health condition that may entitle them to *Reasonable Adjustments* and *Disabled Students' Allowance (DSA)*. Managed by the *Wellbeing and Disability* service.

**DSA — Disabled Students' Allowance**
Government funding to meet the extra costs a disabled student faces in studying. DSA entitlements inform the *Reasonable Adjustments* that the institution implements.

---

## E

**Effective Dating** — see *Bitemporal*

**Effective-Dated / Effective From / Effective To**
Describes a record that states when it became (or will become) true, distinct from when someone entered it into the system (see *Bitemporal*). "Effective-dated" appears throughout this library as shorthand for "carries the date the fact applies from, not just the date it was recorded."

**Enrolment**
The formal act by which an applicant becomes a student of the institution, creating the legal and academic relationship between them. Enrolment is recorded in the SIS and triggers downstream events: fee liability, SLC notification, UKVI CAS creation, and IAM account provisioning.

**Exam Board**
The formal academic governance body responsible for reviewing, confirming, and ratifying student marks and academic decisions. In UK HE, operates at two levels:
- **Module Assessment Board**: confirms and moderates individual module marks.
- **Award and Progression Board**: determines degree classifications, progression decisions, resit requirements, and final awards.
Exam Board decisions are authoritative. Post-ratification, records are locked and may only be amended through a formal appeal or correction process.

**Exceptional Circumstances (EC)**
A student's formal claim that their performance in a specific assessment or period was adversely affected by circumstances outside their control (illness, bereavement, personal crisis). An EC claim is submitted for a specific event; it is distinct from a *Reasonable Adjustment* (which is ongoing). If upheld, the EC outcome is flagged against the relevant module and surfaced to the Exam Board for consideration.

**External Examiner**
An independent academic appointed from another UK HEI to provide quality assurance for a programme or subject area. Scrutinises pre-board calculations, reviews sampled work, attends the Exam Board, and submits an annual report. External examiner confirmation is required before board ratification.

---

## F

**Fee Liability**
The amount a student owes to the institution for tuition. Derived from their enrolment (programme, mode, year of entry). The SIS creates and maintains the fee liability record; the Finance system manages billing and payment.

**Full-Time Equivalent (FTE)**
A measure of student intensity. A full-time student studying a standard 120-credit year is 1.0 FTE. Used in HESA returns and OfS reporting.

**FES — Further Education Sector**
Colleges that deliver further education (below-degree-level, vocational and access provision), as distinct from the higher education sector this glossary otherwise describes. Some Scottish statutory collections (see *SFC*) cover both universities and FE colleges under shared schemas.

---

## G

**Genuine Student Requirement**
A UKVI test applied to Student-route applicants and continuing students: evidence that the person genuinely intends to study and complete their course, as distinct from using the visa route for another purpose. Assessed alongside academic progression and English-language evidence before a CAS is created or renewed.

**GTA — Graduate Teaching Assistant**
A postgraduate student who undertakes paid teaching or demonstrating duties. The SIS records the student's dual role; HR and Payroll manage the employment relationship.

**Graduation**
The ceremony and process by which a student formally receives their award. Graduation records are held in the SIS.

---

## H

**HEAR — Higher Education Achievement Record**
A standardised transcript format endorsed by the UK HE sector, documenting a student's full academic record including marks, awards, and co-curricular activities. Generated by the SIS at the point of graduation.

**HESA — Higher Education Statistics Agency**
The statutory body that collects and publishes data about UK HE. Institutions submit an annual Student Record return to HESA covering all enrolled students. HESA assigns a unique student identifier (HESA ID) to each student.

**Honours Degree**
A bachelor's degree programme that includes a substantial research or advanced study component, typically at Level 6. Awards a *Degree Classification*. Distinguished from a *Pass Degree* (no honours).

---

## I

**Idempotent / Idempotency**
A property of an operation or message where repeating it produces the same result as doing it once — retrying a failed submission, or receiving the same message twice, never creates a duplicate record or a double payment. Achieved using an idempotency key: a value both sides agree identifies "this same attempt," so a repeat is recognised and safely ignored rather than reprocessed.

**ILR — Individualised Learner Record**
England's further-education-sector data return, administered separately from *HESA*. Some providers deliver both HE and FE provision and may source part of their statutory return from ILR rather than the SIS; see the relevant regulatory-reporting process for where this applies.

**Intermission**
A formal, approved temporary suspension of a student's studies, typically for health, personal, or compassionate reasons. The student's enrolment status changes to Intermitting; their fee liability and SLC notifications are adjusted accordingly. Also called *Leave of Absence* at some institutions.

**IRIS**
Wales' higher education student data collection system, used alongside HESA data to compile the statutory return to *Medr*. Referenced here as the applicable Welsh data-quality mapping; institutions should confirm current IRIS specification details directly with Medr.

---

## J

**Jisc**
The UK organisation that provides shared digital infrastructure for education and research, including the platform that runs local and national validation on HESA returns before they reach HESA itself. A HESA return typically passes through Jisc's validation before HESA's own acceptance.

---

## L

**Level**
The academic demand level of a module or award, defined by the Framework for Higher Education Qualifications (FHEQ):
- Level 4: first year of undergraduate study
- Level 5: second year of undergraduate study
- Level 6: third/final year of undergraduate study (including honours)
- Level 7: postgraduate taught (Master's)
- Level 8: doctoral

**Loan** — see *SLC*

---

## M

**Mark**
A numerical or grade score assigned to a student's performance on an assessment component or module. Marks are recorded in the SIS from various sources and aggregated to produce module results.

**Matriculation**
The formal act of joining a university as a member of that institution, historically marked by a ceremony. Some institutions — Scottish universities particularly — use "matriculation" as their term for what this library calls annual re-registration; the underlying outcome (confirming a continuing student for the year ahead) is the same even where local terminology differs.

**Medr**
The Welsh higher education funding and regulation body (formerly the Higher Education Funding Council for Wales, HEFCW), responsible for regulating and funding HE providers in Wales and receiving Wales' statutory HE return. Equivalent in role to the *OfS* in England and the *SFC* in Scotland.

**Misconduct** — see *Academic Integrity*

**Mode of Study**
How a student attends their programme: Full-time, Part-time, Distance/Online, Sandwich (includes a placement year).

**Moderation**
The process of sampling and checking a set of raw marks against departmental and *External Examiner* standards before they are used to calculate module results, to catch marking that is out of line with agreed standards before it affects a student's outcome. Distinct from *Ratification*, which happens later at the Exam Board.

**Module**
The basic unit of academic delivery and credit in UK HE. A discrete, assessed subject taught within a programme. Each module has a credit value, level, assessment structure, and learning outcomes. Students register for specific modules; passing a module earns the associated credits.

**Module Assessment Board** — see *Exam Board*

**Module Diet**
The specific set of modules actually on offer to a given route, level and period — not to be confused with dietary meaning. A student's module choice is validated against their applicable diet, not against every module the institution runs.

**Module Mark**
The final mark for a module, derived by aggregating the marks for all assessment components according to configured weightings. The module mark determines whether the student has passed, failed, or is eligible for compensation/condonement.

---

## O

**OfS — Office for Students**
The independent regulator of higher education in England. Sets regulatory conditions (including the B3 student outcome and experience conditions) that institutions must meet to remain registered. Institutions report outcome data to OfS.

---

## P

**Period**
A sub-division of the academic year during which teaching and assessment occur. Common structures: two semesters, three terms. Modules are delivered within specific periods.

**Personal Tutor**
An academic member of staff assigned to a student to provide academic and pastoral guidance throughout their studies. The SIS records the personal tutor assignment.

**Postgraduate Research (PGR)**
Study leading to a research degree (MPhil, PhD, EngD, etc.) by the completion and examination of an original research thesis, under the guidance of a supervisory team rather than through taught modules. PGR students have distinct progression milestones: **confirmation of registration** (an early review confirming the student can continue), **upgrade** (moving from a lower-level registration, typically MPhil, to PhD once early work shows sufficient promise), thesis submission, and the *viva*. PGR students are also tracked in the *CRIS* as researchers.

**Postgraduate Taught (PGT)**
Study leading to a taught postgraduate qualification (MSc, MA, PGCert, PGDip, etc.) delivered primarily through taught modules and a dissertation.

**Prerequisites**
Module conditions (e.g. prior module passes, credit thresholds) that a student must satisfy before registering for a module. Enforced by the SIS at registration.

**Programme**
A structured, approved course of study leading to an award. A programme consists of a set of modules (core and optional), organised across academic years and levels, governed by programme regulations. In UK HE, broadly equivalent to a "course" or "degree programme".

**Programme Regulations**
The formal rules governing assessment, progression, and award decisions for a programme. Include: pass marks, credit thresholds, classification algorithms, compensation/condonement rules, and resit entitlements. Configured in the SIS as versioned, bitemporal institutional rules.

**Progression**
The determination, typically at the end of an academic year, of whether a student has met the requirements to advance to the next year of their programme. The outcome is one of: Progress, Resit (with or without progression), Repeat Year, Withdraw.

**Provenance**
The recorded trail of where a piece of data came from and how it reached its current state: which source system or person supplied it, when, and under what version of the rules in force at the time. Provenance is what lets a later reviewer answer "why does the record say this?" without having to guess.

---

## Q

**Quarantine**
Holding an incoming record or message that fails validation (an unrecognised course code, an unresolved identity match) in a separate, owned worklist rather than guessing at a value or silently discarding it. A quarantined item blocks only itself; it does not stop other records from processing.

---

## R

**Ratification**
The formal act by which the *Exam Board* approves and records academic decisions (marks, progressions, classifications, awards) as authoritative. Post-ratification, the records are locked and can only be amended through a formal appeal or correction process.

**Reconciliation**
The process of confirming that two systems agree about a shared fact after an exchange between them — for example, checking that a downstream system actually applied an outcome the SIS sent it, or that a regulator's response matches what was submitted. Reconciliation surfaces disagreements as an owned action rather than assuming a message that was sent was also received and acted on correctly.

**Reasonable Adjustment**
An ongoing accommodation made for a student with a disability or long-term health condition, as required by the Equality Act 2010. Examples: extended submission deadlines, examination accommodations (extra time, separate room, rest breaks), alternative assessment formats, accessible content. Managed by the Wellbeing and Disability service; the SIS receives approved outcomes and is the sole distribution point to downstream systems.

**Record Lock**
The state of an academic record that has been ratified by an Exam Board. A locked record cannot be modified except through an approved appeal or correction workflow.

**Resit**
An additional assessment opportunity granted to a student who failed a module, subject to programme regulations. A resit mark is recorded separately from the original mark; some institutions cap the resit mark at the pass mark.

**Registry**
The administrative office responsible for student records, enrolment, regulatory compliance, and examination administration. Registry staff are the primary users of the SRS for administrative functions.

---

## S

**SAAS — Student Awards Agency Scotland**
The Scottish equivalent of the *SLC*: administers tuition-fee and living-cost support for Scottish-domiciled students, and receives enrolment confirmations to trigger and continue funding, in addition to (not instead of) UK-wide SLC collections for cross-border students.

**Share Code**
A time-limited code generated by a person through the UK Home Office's online immigration status service, which an institution can use to check someone's right to study without seeing their underlying immigration documents. Used as identity/right-to-study evidence for applicants and students who hold digital (rather than physical) immigration status.

**Segregation of Duties**
A control requiring that no single person can both carry out and approve the same sensitive action — for example, the person who validates a CAS request's evidence is not the same person who approves the CAS assignment. Intended to catch errors or misuse that a single reviewer might miss or wave through.

**SFC — Scottish Funding Council**
Scotland's higher and further education funding and regulation body, equivalent in role to the *OfS* in England, *Medr* in Wales, and the *DfE* in Northern Ireland. Receives Scotland's statutory HE return, which covers both universities and the *FES* (further education colleges) under shared schemas.

**SLC — Student Loans Company**
The government body that administers student tuition fee loans and maintenance loans on behalf of the UK government. The SIS confirms enrolment to the SLC to trigger loan disbursement, and notifies the SLC of enrolment changes.

**Sponsor Licence**
The licence held by a UK HEI that permits it to sponsor international students under the Student visa route. The institution has obligations under the licence including attendance monitoring and reporting non-compliance to UKVI.

**Student**
An individual who is enrolled on a programme at the institution. The student record in the SIS is the authoritative source of their identity, enrolment, academic progress, and outcomes.

**Student Number**
A human-readable, institution-assigned unique identifier for a student. Distinct from the system UUID and the HESA student identifier.

**Supervisor** — see *Research Supervisor*

---

## T

**Tenant**
An institution (or organisational unit) operating as an independent, isolated entity within a multi-tenant deployment of the SRS. Each tenant has its own users, configuration, integration adapters, and data, all isolated from other tenants at the database layer.

**Transcript**
An official document summarising a student's academic record: modules studied, marks, credits, and awards. Produced by the SRS; see also *HEAR*.

---

## U

**UCAS — Universities and Colleges Admissions Service**
The centralised admissions service for UK HE. Processes undergraduate applications, manages offers and acceptances, and operates the Clearing process for late admissions. The SIS exchanges data with UCAS at multiple points in the admissions lifecycle.

**UKVI — UK Visas and Immigration**
The Home Office agency responsible for immigration control, including the student visa route. Institutions report to UKVI under their sponsor licence obligations.

---

## V

**Valid Time** — see *Bitemporal*

**Viva Voce (Viva)**
An oral examination, typically used to examine a research thesis. The viva outcome determines the final result of a research degree: pass, pass with minor corrections, pass with major corrections, resubmission, fail.

---

## W

**Wellbeing and Disability**
The institutional service responsible for supporting students with disability, mental health, and personal wellbeing needs. Manages *Reasonable Adjustment* and *Exceptional Circumstances* processes; transmits approved outcomes to the SIS.

**Withdrawal**
The permanent end of a student's enrolment, either at the student's request or on the institution's initiative (e.g. academic failure, non-payment). The SIS records the withdrawal, updates fee liability, notifies SLC, and, where applicable, notifies UKVI.
